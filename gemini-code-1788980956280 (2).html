Stern-Gerlach Formalism

Spin-1/2 Qubit ($|z+\rangle$, $|z-\rangle$ basis):

SG1 (|z+⟩): Filter state to |z+⟩ SG2 (|θ+⟩): P = |⟨θ+|z+⟩|² = cos²(θ/2) SG3 (|z+⟩): P = |⟨z+|θ+⟩|² = cos²(θ/2)

Total Throughput: $P_{total} = \frac{1}{2} \cos^4(\theta/2)$

Polarization Formalism

Photon Vector Field ($|V\rangle$, $|H\rangle$ basis):

Pol 1 (Vertical): Intensity I₁ = I₀ / 2 Pol 2 (Angle α): Malus' Law I₂ = I₁ cos²(α) Pol 3 (Vertical): Malus' Law I₃ = I₂ cos²(α)

Total Intensity: $I_{total} = \frac{1}{2} \cos^4(\alpha)$

Key Insights & Isomorphism:
  • Both systems are two-level quantum systems (Qubits) isomorphic to $SU(2) / SO(3)$.
  • The $2\times$ Spatial Mapping: A spin rotation of angle $\theta$ in physical space corresponds to an optical polarizer rotation of $\alpha = \theta / 2$. This is because Spin-1/2 state vectors require a $360^\circ$ spatial rotation to invert sign, whereas photon vector fields repeat after $180^\circ$.
  • Quantum Measurement Paradox: If the middle filter is orthogonal ($\theta = 180^\circ, \alpha = 90^\circ$), 0% of particles pass. Inserting a diagonal middle filter ($\theta = 90^\circ, \alpha = 45^\circ$) increases final output from 0% to 12.5%! The middle filter resets the quantum state vector.
