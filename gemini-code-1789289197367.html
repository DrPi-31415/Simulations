How it works:

The Einstein-de Haas effect demonstrates that magnetic moments (electron spins) possess mechanical angular momentum. When you apply a magnetic field, the microscopic electron spins in the ferromagnetic cylinder align with the field.

Due to the conservation of angular momentum, if the internal spin angular momentum changes direction, the macroscopic cylinder must physically rotate in the opposite direction to compensate. The cylinder is suspended by a torsion wire, which acts as a spring, causing it to twist and eventually oscillate back to rest.
